The Insult Menu

Served by the pound · No substitutions
  • Shlemielשלעמיל

    The clumsy one who spills the soup. “A shlemiel falls on his back and breaks his nose.”

  • Shlimazlשלימזל

    The unlucky one the soup lands on. Never stand next to a shlemiel.

  • Nudnikנודניק

    A pest who calls you three times about the same thing, then asks why you sound tired.

  • Shmendrikשמענדריק

    A nobody who thinks he's a somebody. Wears a tie to take out the garbage.

  • Kvetshקוועטש

    A chronic complainer. The soup is too hot, and also there isn't enough of it.

  • Nebekhנעבעך

    A sad sack. When a nebekh leaves the room, it feels like someone came in.

  • Ganefגנב

    A thief, or a charming crook. Also said fondly of a grandchild who steals the last rugelach.

  • Shnorerשנאָרער

    A freeloader who arrives empty-handed and leaves with the leftovers and your Tupperware.

  • Khazerחזיר

    Literally “pig.” A glutton who takes four pieces of brisket “to be polite.”

  • Klotsקלאָץ

    A block of wood. Clumsy in body and in conversation.

  • Meshugenerמשוגענער

    A crazy person. Usually your uncle, usually about the parking.

  • Trombenikטראָמבעניק

    A blowhard. All trumpet, no tune.

  • Shmegegge—

    A drip, a nothing, and also the nonsense he talks. Pure American Yiddish.

  • Farbisenerפֿאַרביסענער

    A sourpuss. Looks like he bit into a lemon in 1974 and never recovered.

How to serve a klole. Deliver it deadpan, ideally while handing someone a plate. The art is in the setup: the longer the blessing, the harder the landing. Most of these were never meant literally. They were how people who had very little control over their lives got the last word.
